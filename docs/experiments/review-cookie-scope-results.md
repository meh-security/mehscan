The twelve Terra hybrid labels are not twelve independently demonstrated deployed
exploits. Four are conditional CSRF mechanisms; one generated-role conclusion
depends on an ordinary API binding inference. The conditions survive in its
reasons and qualified workspace proposition. Examples:

- The URL-upload write is reported exploitable when the browser sends the accepted
  cookie, including an applicable same-site cross-origin context. Cross-site cookie
  restrictions are explicitly retained. The profile and erasure conclusions also
  retain cookie-delivery premises; erasure concerns a deletion-request record,
  not demonstrated completed account deletion.
- Sol proves the same local lack of an intent check and actual issuer call options,
  but asks what browser conditions allow cross-site delivery and leaves that
  applicability question unresolved. Its shared cookie fact explicitly excludes
  effective library defaults and all other issuers.
- Terra's question asks how the cookie is issued and what controls apply before
  each write. It answers those source questions while recording delivery as a
  condition. The distinction is question/closure level, not lost earlier facts.

The conditional same-site premise is technically meaningful: site and origin are
different boundaries, and SameSite protection does not isolate all origins within
a site. The evaluator verified this against [MDN's CSRF reference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Attacks/CSRF)
and [the browser-platform explanation of site versus origin](https://web.dev/articles/same-site-same-origin).
No attacker-controlled same-site origin, supported browser fleet or deployed
attack was established in this experiment. These references are evaluator checks,
not model evidence. Preserve that applicability boundary in any reader-facing
finding count or severity display.

For generated roles, Sol explicitly relies on normal finale-rest CRUD binding.
Terra native preserves an unresolved effective create-binding question; Terra's
hybrid native fragment reports privileged role assignment without making its
API-contract assumption equally explicit. Keep the candidate and the actual
role/credential/consumer evidence, and flag the missing inference qualification.
Do not upgrade it to directly inspected generator implementation or force the
native uncertainty into an issue merely to equalize labels.

Severity also has conditions. Both Sol paths use medium for coupon, field
disclosure and checkout. Terra uses high for sensitive fields with a conditional
cross-origin JSONP chain; native basket read is medium, hybrid high. These are
calibration/scope differences, not automatic evidence of additional mechanisms.
Adjudication remains implementation-agent source review, not independent gold.
